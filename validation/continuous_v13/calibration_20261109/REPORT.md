# Continuous v1.3: aerial calibration and stress checks

## Motivation and change

The 85-match follow-up to the previous checkpoint contained 1,432 headed shots attributed to crosses (16.85 per match). Those shots scored 19.9% of the time with mean model xG 9.4%; these are model diagnostics, not a direct header-for-header reference comparison. Existing aerial first-contact control applied only a small incoming-speed penalty, while the execution model did not use the speed of the ball at head contact.

This experimental revision makes aerial control depend more strongly on incoming ball speed, nearby pressure, heading skill and surface conditions. The contact frame retains incoming speed; directed header dispersion also increases with incoming speed and pressure and decreases with heading skill. Ground reception and the situation xG estimator are unchanged. The optional dispersion multiplier defaults to 1 in event-mode geometry. These are provisional model assumptions, not measurements of real aerial-contact mechanics; there are no quotas on shots, goals or results.

## Experimental design

The development pilot ran 24 paired synthetic matches per version (fixture indices 0–23, seeds 124000–124023). After choosing the revision, a held-out comparison ran 100 paired matches per version (indices 24–123, seeds 137024–137123). Team fixtures, styles, generic strength-80 rosters and seeds match within each pair. The fixture schedule comes from EPL 2015/16, but the players are generic, so this evaluates distributions rather than predicting actual results. Earlier aggregate league results were already available during development, and fixtures are from the same league: the held-out seeds are independent of the pilot, not a new league or an entirely unseen research setting.

All 248 simulated matches completed without recorded failures; the observer-parity option passed for one held-out fixture in each version. `pilot_*.json.gz` and `holdout_*.json.gz` retain compact match summaries and shot frames, not full tracking or full event ledgers. The per-shard source hashes, engine code, physical-model and shot-model hashes are recorded inside the archives. The modified shared `spatial_shot_v13.py` hash is `71a728c7dfd751b97649dedf4f4adec11f2e4ec8eadaa4c6de09afd1acca0ff9`. The JSON summaries are reproducible with `compare_paired_runs.py`; the held-out summary was regenerated byte-for-byte from the compact archives. Bootstrap intervals use 5,000 paired resamples, with a fixed analysis seed.

The event-source definitions and shot contexts are approximations and may not exactly match StatsBomb categories. EPL shot counts include penalties while the published geometry reference excludes them. The 100-match paired mean differences were -2.03 goals (bootstrap 95% interval [-2.74, -1.36]) and -9.80 shots ([-11.70, -7.95]). The penalty-shot difference was -0.13 ([-0.40, 0.14]); the sample does not establish a reduction. Pass accuracy, pass volume and shot-distance agreement worsened. Real-game reference numbers are descriptive and are not used as in-engine quotas.

Revised open-play chance quality remains high: mean non-penalty model xG per shot was 0.183 versus 0.092 in the reference; simulated conversion was 0.176 versus 0.093. The 100-match home/away/draw percentages were 40/45/15, compared with 45/44/11 before; this is a small sample with generic teams and does not establish real home-advantage calibration.

## Foul investigation and remaining work

An additional eight exploratory synthetic games recorded 183 physical-foul decisions: eight were in the penalty area and all eight produced penalties; the median attack-coordinate x of those box fouls was 92.34 m. These are genuine in-box locations under the current coordinate conversion, not evidence by themselves that the foul probability or the player's position is realistic. The sample is too small to infer rates reliably. The diagnostic and its limitations are in `foul_diagnostic.json`; no penalty-rate cap was introduced.

The model also generated many aerial long balls and too many high-quality close-range chances. A causal review should cover formation geometry, time spent in the area, local contact rates and competition for aerial deliveries, as well as pass-type classification and expected pass success. Freeze new parameters before a larger evaluation, and use a different league and observed tracking where possible. This revision must remain experimental; it does not certify real-world realism.

## Correctness and reproducibility

- 177 targeted unit tests passed locally, covering continuous physics, state, aerial play, refereeing, offside, and the event-mode geometry.
- Four synthetic matches were saved at approximately 25, 40, 55 and 70 minutes. Each restored match was observed while running to completion and produced the same full JSON-normalized final state as its uninterrupted counterpart. JSON normalization accounts for tuple/list representation of historical event points; it does not prove all save/load cases or versions.
- One full match per held-out variant passed observer parity. Results are retained in `resume_checks.json` and the hold-out summary.
- No official final seed was inspected or executed; `main` was not changed.

The existing advantage fix and its tests remain part of the draft PR. CI runs the continuous unit suite, independent matches and a full save/resume check on Python 3.11 and 3.12.
